## v0.8.1-A — fix: effects were hidden inside the model they were played on
- Agent: A (Claude) · Date: 2026-09-19
- Report: in the owner's first Pixelfork-generated 3D game only a debug explosion (big, in open air above the hero) was visible; every real effect was invisible. The setup was fine. My own player never showed this because I click on surfaces and use anchors outside the body.
- Fix: `autoPull()`. At spawn, one ray camera → effect point finds the model the point is inside of (a visible mesh whose box contains it, then up its parents while they stay model-sized, so a hero made of parts counts as one and a huge `enemies` group does not). World-aligned (`in3d`) parts are not moved. New `VFX.debug = true` logs every play.
- Two wrong turns on the way, kept here so nobody repeats them: (1) a fixed pull distance is always wrong for some model size; (2) clearance measured along the ray to the point is wrong for anything off-centre on screen, because billboards are perpendicular to where the camera faces, not to that ray. Also a test trap: the demo's cubes walk toward the camera, so with only VFX time frozen they walked into the flash and looked like a geometry bug.
- Tested in the new `demo/three-cdn.html` (a minimal AI-style game, camera following a hero 70 m from the origin): measured pulls big cube 1.38–1.41, same cube nested in a huge group 1.16→ same as ungrouped, hero with an arm in front 0.98, coin 0.47, open air / floor / behind a wall 0.06, `pull: false` 0. ## v0.6.3-A — fix: ribbon trails were fat bands with hard-cut ends
- Agent: A (Claude) · Date: 2026-09-19
- Bug (owner's two screenshots): `magic.fire` / `move.trail` in 3D showed a wide glowing band, soft on its long edges, that stopped with a hard vertical cut. In perspective it looked like a slanted rectangle. I first misdiagnosed it as the draw-order tie fixed in v0.6.2 (that tie was real, but it was not this).
- Cause, all in `c.ribbon()`: (1) `WidthOverLength` REPLACES the width with the curve's raw value in world units, it does not scale `size`; my 0→1 curve made a ribbon up to 1 m wide whatever `width` said. (2) Its curve runs from the newest point (t=0, at the mover) to the oldest (t=1), so my taper was backwards: thin at the mover, fattest at the tail end. (3) Nothing faded along the length: the `ribbon` texture only faded across the width, so the tail ended at full opacity.
- Fix: the curve is now built from the real width (`width × u`), full at t=0 and zero at t=1; the `ribbon` texture is multiplied by a ramp along u (u=0 is the tail end) so the tail dissolves.
- Tested: recorded widths along a 22-point ribbon: 0.60 m at the mover → 0.37 mid → 0.03 at the tail (expected full width 0.60). Looked at a fireball and a blue trail flying arcs through the air: tapered, fading comet tails, no hard edges. Both retire after stop().

## v0.6.2-A — fix: glows sliced into hard-edged shapes in 3D
- Agent: A (Claude) · Date: 2026-09-19
- Bug (owner's screenshot): a burning effect's base glow was cut into a slanted rectangle while its flames were fine, and only at some camera positions. Cause: the player's walls are transparent materials (for the see-through window), so three.js sorts them in the same pass as the particle batches. Walls and the order-0 particle batch tied on `renderOrder` and the tie is broken by object distance, which flips as the camera moves. When a wall BEHIND the effect was drawn after it, it painted over the glow (particles do not write depth); the glow survived only where the opaque floor was behind it.
- Fix (library, not the player): in scene mode every system's `renderOrder` is offset by `S.renderOrder` (default 1000, `useScene({ renderOrder })` to change), so particles always draw after a game's transparent objects (glass, water, fading walls). 2D overlay unchanged.
- Tested: particle batches now report render orders 999–1004 against the walls' 0; the burning-crate case renders a full round glow with the far walls behind it.
